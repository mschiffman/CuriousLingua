Not Feeling Well

かぜ・ずつう・ぎっくりごし

A slight fever that will not stop a man from going to the office, a headache and grandmother's cures, a back thrown out by a sneeze, and an appetite that comes back for ramen only: four short scenes between Emi and Riku. Listen for ga itai when something hurts, for the advice with hō ga ii, and for how you say you feel a little better.

Notes

  • Pain: part of the body + ga itai. Atama ga itai (head), nodo ga itai (throat), koshi ga itai (lower back), onaka ga itai (stomach).
  • Feeling: guai ga warui (I feel unwell), sukoshi yoku natta (I'm a little better), genki (well).
  • A cold: kaze. Netsu ga aru (I have a fever), seki ga deru (I'm coughing), hanamizu ga deru (my nose is running).
  • Temperature: 37.2 is sanjūnana-do ni-bu. A slight fever is binetsu.
  • Advice: past form + hō ga ii. Byōin ni itta hō ga ii: you should see a doctor.
  • Medicine is drunk: kusuri o nomu. Ichinichi sankai, shokugo ni: three times a day, after meals.
  • Can and can't: arukeru, arukenai (walk); ugokeru, ugokenai (move).
