Hello and Goodbye

おはよう・またね

Good morning at noon, a trip to the convenience store in pajamas, a husband who comes home still talking to his colleagues, and a long goodbye between two people who live together: four short scenes between Emi and Riku. Listen for which greeting goes with which moment, and for what happens when someone picks the wrong one.

Role play: I am Press Play all. Say your lines in the pauses.

The audio for this lesson is not recorded yet.

Notes

  • By time of day: ohayō in the morning, konnichiwa in the daytime, konbanwa in the evening, oyasumi at bedtime.
  • To be polite, add to the casual form: ohayō gozaimasu, oyasuminasai, okaerinasai.
  • Home greetings come in pairs. Leaving: ittekimasu, then itterasshai. Coming back: tadaima, then okaeri.
  • Office greetings: otsukaresama desu all day long; osaki ni shitsurei shimasu when you leave first; otsukaresama deshita in reply.
  • Between friends: ja, mata ne; mata ashita; bai bai. Sayōnara is rare in daily life: it sounds like a long goodbye.
  • Casual speech drops desu and masu and ends in yo, ne or yo ne: hiru da yo, hayakatta ne, kaeru yo ne.
