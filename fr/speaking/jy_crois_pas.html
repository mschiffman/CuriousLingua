J'y crois pas !

Four ways to say you can't believe it

An angel expected in the garden, a lemon syrup that gets thinner in the sun, a dangerous belly button and a great debate between six-year-olds: four true stories from a childhood in Yangon, told by Emma. Listen for how Lucas shows he cannot believe it, and how Emma insists that it is true.

Role play: I am Press Play all. Say your lines in the pauses.

The audio for this lesson is not recorded yet.

Notes

  • Ah bon ?, Sérieux ? and Sans blague ? show surprise and invite the speaker to go on.
  • Tu plaisantes ?, Tu rigoles ? and T'es pas sérieux / sérieuse ! say: I think you are joking.
  • C'est pas possible !, C'est pas vrai ! and J'en reviens pas ! say: I can't believe it. They do not call anyone a liar.
  • N'importe quoi ! and Mon œil ! do: they say the other person is talking nonsense.
  • To insist: Je te jure ! Je te mens pas. And before a surprising story: Tu vas pas le croire.
  • Imparfait for what used to happen (ma mère faisait du sirop), passé composé for what happened once (une voisine a essayé).
  • Spoken French drops ne: c'est pas vrai, j'y crois pas, je te mens pas.
