D'accord !

Four ways to agree

A deal about the dishes, a haircut nobody noticed, a shelf built without the instructions and a morning run that turns into croissants: four short scenes between Emma and Lucas. Listen for how they say yes, and for the moments when yes means 'I don't believe you'.

Role play: I am Press Play all. Say your lines in the pauses.

The audio for this lesson is not recorded yet.

Notes

  • D'accord says yes to a plan. Je suis d'accord agrees with an opinion. On est d'accord seals it.
  • Ça marche, carrément, grave and c'est clair are casual. With a boss, say tout à fait or absolument.
  • Si replaces oui after a negative question: Tu remarques rien ? — Si !
  • Tone can flip the meaning: c'est ça, oui… and si tu le dis… say 'I don't believe you'.
  • T'as raison gives in. Justement does the opposite: it uses the other person's point against them.
  • Spoken French drops ne: tu remarques rien, je porte pas, y aura pas.
