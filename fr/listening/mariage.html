Vingt-cinq ans de mariage

A silver wedding anniversary in the kitchen

Élise and Thomas have been married for twenty-five years. Thomas is cooking, Élise is teasing him, and the two trade jokes about their marriage before planning tomorrow's trip to Costco. Quick, dry humour in everyday spoken French.

How to use this lesson

  1. Press Play all with the text hidden. Listen for the gist only: what are they celebrating, what is Thomas cooking, and what will they do tomorrow?
  2. Listen again, then answer the questions under the dialogue.
  3. Tap the lines to show the text, or switch off Hide text. Listen once more while reading.
  4. Shadow it: play a line, press Pause (or the space bar), repeat it aloud, then Resume.
  5. Read the notes on the jokes and on spoken French, then take a role and say the lines in the pauses.
Role play: I am Press Play all. Say your lines in the pauses.

Notes

  • Le plus dur est fait = the hardest part is done. Élise plays on dur: avoir la tête dure (or être une tête dure) means to be stubborn. Moi qui croyais que… = and here I was thinking that…
  • Tout est dur avec toi: here dur means difficult. Thomas turns her joke back on her.
  • Vingt-cinq ans… à peine un quart de siècle: the joke is that 25 years IS a quarter of a century. The anniversaries are les noces: noces d’argent (25 years), noces d’or (50 years).
  • Je n’ai jamais été avec l’homme de ma vie: Thomas takes « l’homme de sa vie » literally. Élise answers with l’échapper belle = to have a narrow escape: lucky him, whoever he is.
  • Spoken shortcuts: faut croire = il faut croire (I guess), faut bien que = il faut bien que, eh ben / bah = eh bien (well), le congélo = le congélateur, du rab = some extra, leftovers (familiar).
  • On va chez Costco ? Use chez with the name of a shop or a company: chez Costco, chez Carrefour, but au supermarché. Faire le plein = to fill up the tank. En sortant = on the way out (en + present participle).
  • rappeler à quelqu’un de faire quelque chose = to remind someone to do something: Rappelle-moi de faire le plein. With two pronouns, te or me comes before le: je te le rappelle, je te l’ai dit.
