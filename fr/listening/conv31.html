Toujours avoir raison

A couple teasing each other at home

Claire calls Marc from the bathroom, finds her lotion mysteriously refilled, and the two tease each other about who is always right. Fast, casual spoken French, full of dropped ne and set phrases.

How to use this lesson

  1. Press Play all with the text hidden. Listen for the gist only: where are they, and what are they teasing each other about?
  2. Listen again, then answer the questions under the dialogue.
  3. Tap the lines to show the text, or switch off Hide text. Listen once more while reading.
  4. Shadow it: play a line, press Pause (or the space bar), repeat it aloud, then Resume.
  5. Read the notes on spoken French, then take a role and say the lines in the pauses.
Role play: I am Press Play all. Say your lines in the pauses.

Notes

  • Spoken French drops the ne: j'en doute pas, je mérite pas, j'y peux rien, je savais pas, c'est pas déjà le cas ? In writing: je n'en doute pas, ce n'est pas.
  • More shortcuts: faut = il faut, y a = il y a, t'as = tu as, ouais = oui.
  • Elle est où, ta femme ? Spoken questions often keep the word order of a statement and put the question word at the end: Elle est où ? = Où est-elle ?
  • être sur son trône = to be on the toilet (joking). passer son tour = to pass, to sit this one out.
  • Tu parles ! = Oh, sure! / You bet! It can mean yes, or, said with irony, the opposite. Ben voyons = Oh, come on (I don't believe you).
  • marrant = drôle. carrément = totally, absolutely. un minois = a pretty little face. C'est de naissance = I was born like that.
  • donner raison à quelqu'un = to say that someone is right. avoir raison = to be right, hence the joke: Toujours Raison.
