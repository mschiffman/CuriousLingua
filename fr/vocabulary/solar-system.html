Solar System — what is accurate here
  • Orbits and positions are real. Each planet follows its true ellipse (size, eccentricity, tilt, orientation) and sits where it really is on the date shown, using NASA/JPL's approximate Keplerian elements (Standish), valid 1800–2050. Typical error is well under 0.1° for the inner planets and a few tenths of a degree at worst for the outer ones.
  • Distances are to scale. 1 AU is the same length everywhere in the scene.
  • Sizes: your choice. "Enlarged" draws planets 400× and the Sun 40× too big so you can see them (planets stay correct relative to each other, but not to the Sun). "True scale" draws everything at its real size — pick a planet to fly to it.
  • Axes are real. Each body's rotation axis points in its true direction in space (IAU pole coordinates), and spins at its true rate and direction — Venus and Uranus backwards.
  • Saturn's rings span their real radii (C ring to A ring, with the Cassini Division).
  • Not real: surface patterns are stylised (only colours are representative), the background stars are decorative, and moons, dwarf planets and asteroids are omitted. "Earth" is plotted at the Earth–Moon barycentre, at most ~4,700 km from Earth's centre.
