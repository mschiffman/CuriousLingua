The past simple

Finished actions in the past

Use it for something that started and finished in the past. We often say when: yesterday, last week, in 2020, two days ago. It is the tense of stories.

When to use itHow to make itSignal wordsNegative: did notQuestions: didPast simple or present perfect?

When to use it

One finished action

It happened, and it is over

  • I visited my aunt yesterday.

  • She bought a new bag last week.

  • We watched a film on Saturday.

  • He lost his keys this morning.

  • They arrived two hours ago.

A story

One thing after another

  • I got up, washed my face and ate breakfast.

  • He opened the door and looked outside.

  • The tiger saw the deer, jumped and missed.

  • She sat down and started to read.

Past habits

Again and again, but not now

  • When I was little, I walked to school every day.

  • We played in the river every summer.

  • My grandfather told us a story every night.

  • She always wore a red hat.

Past states

How things were

  • I was very tired last night.

  • The streets were empty.

  • He had a small dog when he was a boy.

  • We lived in a village for ten years.

How to make it

Regular verbs

verb + -ed (the same for every subject)

  • Most verbs: add -ed
    play → played, watch → watched, visit → visited.
  • Verb ends in -e: add -d
    like → liked, live → lived, dance → danced.
  • Consonant + y: change y to -ied
    study → studied, carry → carried, cry → cried.
  • Short verb, one vowel + one consonant: double the last letter
    stop → stopped, plan → planned, clap → clapped.

I played, you played, she played, they played: there is no -s in the past.

Irregular verbs

Many common verbs do not take -ed. You have to learn them.

The verb be

I / he / she / it was · you / we / they were

  • I was at home.

  • She was happy.

  • They were late.

  • We were in the same class.

Signal words

yesterday, last night / week / month / year, two days ago, a long time ago, in 2020, on Monday, this morning (when the morning is over), when I was little, then, after that …

Negative: did not

did not (didn't) + verb

  • I didn't see him.

  • She didn't go to school yesterday.

  • We didn't have time.

  • They didn't like the film.

  • He wasn't at home. They weren't ready.

    With be: wasn't, weren't.

After didn't, use the base verb: I didn't go, not I didn't went.

Questions: did

Did + subject + verb ?

  • Did you see the film?

    Yes, I did. / No, I didn't.

  • Where did she go?

  • What did you eat for breakfast?

  • When did they arrive?

  • Were you at school yesterday? Was it cold?

    With be, put was or were first.

Past simple or present perfect?

  • Past simple: a finished time. I lost my pen yesterday.

    We know when. The story is in the past.

  • Present perfect: no time, and it matters now. I have lost my pen.

    I do not have it now.

  • Not together: I have seen him yesterday. ✗ → I saw him yesterday. ✓

    With yesterday, last week, ago: always the past simple.
