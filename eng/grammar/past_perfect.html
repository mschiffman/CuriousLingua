The past perfect

The past before the past

You are telling a story in the past, and you need to go one step further back. The past perfect shows which action happened first: When we arrived, the film had started.

When to use itHow to make itSignal wordsNegative: hadn'tQuestionsPast perfect or past simple?

When to use it

Earlier past

It happened before another past action

  • When we arrived, the film had already started.

  • She had left when I called.

  • I had finished my homework before dinner.

  • The train had gone when we got to the station.

  • By the time he woke up, everyone had eaten.

The reason

Why something was so in the past

  • I was tired because I had not slept well.

  • He was happy because he had passed the test.

  • The ground was wet. It had rained in the night.

  • She couldn't call. She had lost her phone.

Experience before then

Never … before that moment

  • I had never seen the sea before that day.

  • It was the best food he had ever eaten.

  • She had never flown before.

  • We had met once before.

What someone said

Reporting the past

  • She said she had seen the film.

    Her words: “I saw the film.”

  • He told me he had lost his bag.

    His words: “I have lost my bag.”

  • They said they had finished.

  • I thought you had gone home.

How to make it

Pattern

had + past participle (the same for every subject)

Full formShort formNegative
I had finishedI'd finishedI hadn't finished
she had goneshe'd goneshe hadn't gone
they had eatenthey'd eatenthey hadn't eaten

The past participle is the same as in the present perfect: finished, gone, seen, eaten, done. Only have / has changes to had.

Two actions, two tenses

  • The earlier action: past perfect. The later action: past simple.
  • The order in the sentence does not matter: I had eaten when he arrived = When he arrived, I had eaten.
  • With before and after the order is already clear, so the past simple is often enough: After I ate, I went out.

Signal words

already, just, never … before, by the time, when, before, after, as soon as, until, because …

Negative: hadn't

had + not + past participle

  • I hadn't seen him before.

  • She hadn't finished when the bell rang.

  • We hadn't eaten, so we were hungry.

  • They hadn't been there before.

Questions

Had + subject + past participle ?

  • Had you met him before?

    Yes, I had. / No, I hadn't.

  • Had the film started when you arrived?

  • Where had she gone?

  • Why had they left so early?

Past perfect or past simple?

  • Past simple + past simple: When I arrived, she left.

    First I arrived. Then she left.

  • Past simple + past perfect: When I arrived, she had left.

    First she left. Then I arrived: I did not see her.

  • One past action only: I went to Bagan last year.

    No earlier action: the past simple is enough. Do not use the past perfect alone.
