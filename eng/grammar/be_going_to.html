Be going to

Plans, and things you can see coming

Use it for a plan you already have, and for something you can see is about to happen. The future starts from something that is true now: your decision, or what you can see.

When to use itHow to make itSignal wordsNegativeQuestionsGoing to or will?

When to use it

Plans

You decided before now

  • I am going to visit my uncle tomorrow.

  • She is going to study medicine.

  • We are going to buy a new house next year.

  • They are going to play football after school.

  • I am going to be a teacher.

You can see it coming

Something now tells you

  • Look at those clouds. It is going to rain.

    I can see the clouds now.

  • Be careful! You are going to fall.

    I can see the danger.

  • The bus is very full. We are going to be late.

  • She is running very fast. She is going to win.

Very soon

It is about to happen

  • Hurry up! The film is going to start.

  • Sit down. I am going to tell you a story.

  • Watch this. I am going to jump.

  • The baby is going to cry.

How to make it

Pattern

am / is / are + going to + verb

Subjectbegoing toverb
Iamgoing toplay
he / she / itisgoing toplay
you / we / theyaregoing toplay

The verb after going to never changes: she is going to play, not she is going to plays.

Speaking

  • People often say the short forms: I'm going to, she's going to, they're going to.
  • In fast speech going to sounds like gonna. You will hear it in songs and films. Do not write it at school.
  • With the verb go, people often just say I'm going to the market tomorrow (not going to go). Both are correct.

Signal words

tomorrow, tonight, next week / month / year, this weekend, soon, later, in two days, after school, when I grow up, Look!, Be careful! …

Negative

am / is / are + not + going to + verb

  • I am not going to tell you.

  • He isn't going to come.

  • We aren't going to wait.

  • It isn't going to rain today.

Questions

Am / Is / Are + subject + going to + verb ?

  • Are you going to help me?

    Yes, I am. / No, I'm not.

  • Is she going to come to the party?

  • What are you going to do tomorrow?

  • Where are they going to live?

  • When is he going to call?

Going to or will?

  • Going to: you decided before. I am going to buy a bike. I have the money.

    It is already your plan.

  • Will: you decide now, while speaking. The phone is ringing. I will answer it.

    There was no plan.

  • Going to: you can see it. Look at the sky. It is going to rain.

    The clouds are there now.

  • Will: you think so. I think it will rain tomorrow.

    It is only your idea.
