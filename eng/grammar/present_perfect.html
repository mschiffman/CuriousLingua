The present perfect

The past that still matters now

It joins the past to now. Something happened before now, but when is not important: the important thing is the result now, or your experience, or that it is still true. Never use it with a finished time such as yesterday.

When to use itHow to make itSignal wordsNegative: haven't / hasn'tQuestionsPresent perfect or past simple?

When to use it

Experience

In your life, up to now: ever, never

  • I have been to Bagan.

  • She has never seen snow.

  • Have you ever eaten durian?

  • We have met before.

  • He has read that book three times.

Result now

Something happened, and you can see or feel it now

  • I have lost my key.

    I can't open the door now.

  • She has broken her arm.

    It is in a cast now.

  • They have gone home.

    They are not here now.

  • Look! Somebody has opened the window.

    It is open now.

Still true

It started in the past and continues: for, since

  • I have lived here for ten years.

  • She has known him since 2020.

  • We have been friends for a long time.

  • He has had that bike since he was six.

  • How long have you studied English?

Just, already, yet

News, and things you are waiting for

  • He has just left.

    A moment ago.

  • I have already done my homework.

    Sooner than you think.

  • She hasn't arrived yet.

    We are still waiting.

  • Have you finished yet?

How to make it

Pattern

have / has + past participle

Subjecthavepast participleShort form
I / you / we / theyhavefinishedI've finished

For regular verbs the past participle is the -ed form: played, finished, lived. For irregular verbs it is the third form: go, went, gone.

For or since? Been or gone?

  • for + how long: for two hours, for ten years, for a long time.
  • since + the starting point: since Monday, since 2020, since I was a child.
  • She has gone to Yangon: she is there now, or on the way.
  • She has been to Yangon: she went and came back. It is her experience.

Signal words

ever, never, just, already, yet, for, since, before, so far, up to now, today, this week / year (when it is not over), recently, How long …?

Negative: haven't / hasn't

have / has + not + past participle

  • I haven't seen that film.

  • She hasn't called yet.

  • We haven't eaten since this morning.

  • He has never been late.

Questions

Have / Has + subject + past participle ?

  • Have you finished?

    Yes, I have. / No, I haven't.

  • Has she arrived yet?

  • Have you ever been to the sea?

  • How long have they lived there?

  • What have you done?

Present perfect or past simple?

  • Present perfect: no time, it matters now. I have seen that film.

    I know the story.

  • Past simple: a finished time. I saw that film last week.

    Last week is over.

  • Present perfect: still true. She has lived here for five years.

    She lives here now.

  • Past simple: finished. She lived here for five years.

    She does not live here now.

  • In a conversation: “Have you ever been to Bagan?” “Yes, I went there in 2022.”

    First the experience (perfect), then the details (past simple).
