# What Time Is It?
type: conversation
course: eng
level: a1
topic: Situations
name: what_time
subtitle: Four conversations about the time
intro: Emma asks the time at a bus stop, plans a film with Lucas, tells him he is late for class, and Lucas buys a train ticket from an agent. Listen for the time in each conversation, then say it with them.
speakers: E = Emma, L = Lucas, G = Gentleman, A = Agent

## At the bus stop
E: Excuse me. Do you have the time, please?
NOTE: A polite way to ask a stranger the time. It means: What time is it?
G: Yes, of course. It's ten fifteen.
NOTE: 10:15. You can also say a quarter past ten.
E: Ten fifteen? Already?
G: Yes. Are you in a hurry?
E: A little. My bus leaves at twenty past ten.
NOTE: 10:20. at + a time: at twenty past ten.
G: Then you'd better hurry! The bus stop is right there.
NOTE: you'd better = you should, now.
E: Thank you. Have a nice day!

## At the cinema
L: Shall we go to the movies tonight?
E: Good idea! What time does the film start?
L: At half past eight.
NOTE: 8:30. You can also say eight thirty.
E: And what time shall we meet?
L: At quarter to eight, outside the cinema?
NOTE: 7:45. to = before: fifteen minutes before eight.
E: Quarter to eight. OK.
L: Great. See you tonight!

## Late for class
L: Emma, what time is it?
E: It's ten to nine.
NOTE: 8:50. Ten minutes before nine.
L: What? Already? I'm late!
E: What time does your class start?
L: At nine o'clock sharp.
NOTE: o'clock is only for the exact hour. Sharp = exactly, not one minute later.
E: Then you've got ten minutes. Hurry!
L: I'm off. See you later!
NOTE: I'm off = I'm leaving now.

## At the station
L: Good afternoon. What time does the next train to New York leave?
A: At two thirty-five, from platform three.
NOTE: 2:35. After half past, we often just say the numbers: two thirty-five.
L: And what time does it arrive?
A: At four forty.
NOTE: 4:40. Four forty, or twenty to five.
L: Perfect. And what time is it now?
A: It's ten past two. You've got twenty-five minutes.
NOTE: 2:10. past = after the hour.
L: Great. One ticket, please.

## notes
- What time is it? / Do you have the time? ask for the time now. The answer starts with It's: It's ten to nine.
- What time does the film start? asks when something happens. The answer uses at: At half past eight.
- past = after the hour: twenty past ten (10:20), ten past two (2:10).
- to = before the next hour: quarter to eight (7:45), ten to nine (8:50).
- You can also say the numbers: ten fifteen (10:15), two thirty-five (2:35), four forty (4:40).
- o'clock is only for the exact hour: nine o'clock, but half past nine.

## questions

- Ten fifteen
* Twenty past ten
- Half past ten
WHY: Emma says: My bus leaves at twenty past ten.
Q: What time do Emma and Lucas meet outside the cinema?
* Quarter to eight
- Half past eight
- Eight o'clock
WHY: Lucas says: At quarter to eight, outside the cinema. The film starts at half past eight.
Q: Emma says: It's ten to nine. What time is it?
- 9:10
- 9:50
* 8:50
WHY: Ten to nine = ten minutes before nine.
Q: It's ten past two. The train leaves at two thirty-five. How long does Lucas have?
- Ten minutes
* Twenty-five minutes
- Thirty-five minutes
WHY: From 2:10 to 2:35 is twenty-five minutes.
