# The Apple That Asked a Question

## Slide 1

Long ago, in a quiet English village, lived a curious young man named Isaac Newton.

Isaac loved asking questions about the world around him.

One sunny afternoon, he sat beneath an apple tree, enjoying the peaceful garden.

## Slide 2

Suddenly, a ripe red apple fell from the tree.

THUMP!

The apple landed on the soft grass beside Isaac.

Isaac looked at the apple and began to wonder.

## Slide 3

"Why did the apple fall down?" he wondered.

"Why didn't it fly up into the sky?"

He picked up the apple and held it high.

Then he let it go.

Down it fell again!

## Slide 4

Isaac began thinking about an invisible force.

What if the Earth was pulling the apple toward itself?

Today, we call this force gravity.

Gravity pulls objects toward the Earth.

That is why apples fall instead of floating away!

## Slide 5

That evening, Isaac looked up at the beautiful Moon.

A wonderful question came into his mind.

"If Earth pulls an apple, could it also pull the Moon?"

But the Moon did not crash into the Earth.

Why not?

## Slide 6

Isaac had a remarkable idea.

The Moon is moving sideways through space.

At the same time, Earth's gravity pulls it inward.

The Moon keeps falling toward the Earth, but keeps missing it!

That is how the Moon stays in orbit around Earth.

## Slide 7

Isaac spent many years studying and calculating.

He discovered that the same force helps explain the movements of planets and their moons.

He developed a mathematical law called universal gravitation.

It helped scientists understand how objects attract one another.

## Slide 8

It all began with a simple question about a falling apple.

Millions of people had seen apples fall before Isaac Newton.

But Isaac wanted to know why they fell.

Sometimes, the biggest discoveries begin with the smallest questions.

So keep looking, keep wondering, and never stop asking, "Why?"
