# Not Feeling Well (English translation)

## A Cold (Kaze)

**Riku:** Emi, I'm not feeling too good.
**Emi:** What's wrong?
**Riku:** My throat hurts. My nose is running. And I've got a cough.
**Emi:** Do you have a fever?
**Riku:** Thirty-seven point two.
**Emi:** That's barely a fever.
**Riku:** Barely a fever… This might be the end for me.
**Emi:** Are you taking the day off work?
**Riku:** No, I'm going.
**Emi:** Then you're fine.

## A Headache (Zutsū)

**Emi:** I have a headache.
**Riku:** Since when?
**Emi:** Since this morning.
**Riku:** Did you take some medicine?
**Emi:** Not yet.
**Riku:** My grandma used to say it helps to wrap a green onion around your neck.
**Emi:** That's for a cold.
**Riku:** Then stick a pickled plum on your forehead.
**Emi:** … I'll take some medicine.
**Riku:** Should I go buy some green onions?
**Emi:** I think it'll go away if you just keep quiet.

## A Bad Back (Gikkuri-goshi)

**Riku:** Ow! My back…!
**Emi:** What happened?
**Riku:** I can't move.
**Emi:** What did you do?
**Riku:** I sneezed.
**Emi:** You sneezed?
**Riku:** It was a big sneeze.
**Emi:** You should go to the doctor.
**Riku:** I'll go tomorrow.
**Emi:** Can you walk?
**Riku:** No.
**Emi:** Then sleep right there tonight. Good night.

## Rice Porridge (Okayu)

**Emi:** How are you feeling?
**Riku:** A little better. But I still don't have an appetite.
**Emi:** I made you some rice porridge.
**Riku:** Thanks. … I want ramen.
**Emi:** So you do have an appetite.
**Riku:** Ramen goes in a different stomach.
**Emi:** Did you take your medicine?
**Riku:** Three times a day, after meals.
**Emi:** Then you're fine. You've got work tomorrow.
**Riku:** … Actually, I think I might still have a fever.

---

## Notes

- **Sanjūnana-do ni-bu (37.2 °C):** about 99 °F. Japanese uses *bu* for tenths of a degree. *Binetsu* means "a slight fever", which is why Emi isn't impressed.
- **Negi around the neck:** an old Japanese home remedy for a cold. You wrap a long green onion (*negi*) around your neck with a cloth.
- **Umeboshi on the forehead:** another folk remedy, this one for headaches. You stick a pickled plum, or its skin, on your temples or forehead.
- **Gikkuri-goshi:** suddenly throwing out your lower back. In English you'd say "I threw my back out."
- **Betsubara:** literally "a separate stomach". It's usually said about dessert: "there's always room for it."
- **Okayu:** soft rice porridge, the usual food for someone who's sick in Japan.
